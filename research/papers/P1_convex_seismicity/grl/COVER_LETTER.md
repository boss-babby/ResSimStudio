Dear Editor,

We submit the Letter "Minimum-Seismicity Injection Design Is Convex, and the Value of Design Is Set by Two Numbers" for consideration in
Geophysical Research Letters.

Injection-rate control is the main operational lever against induced seismicity, yet published studies disagree on whether constant,
step-up or tapered schedules are safest. Under the widely used Dieterich seismicity-rate model with linear poroelastic coupling, we show
that the expected event count is convex in the injection-rate histories of all wells. The convexity step itself is short. Its
consequences are what we consider new and useful:
* certified global optima and a unique optimal stress history;
* an explanation of part of the disagreement, because protocol rankings flip with the counting window;
* two dimensionless numbers, Π and θ, that indicate in advance whether optimisation can pay at all.

Recent control-theory work, including a study of the same Otaniemi stimulation (Kim et al., 2025, Geothermics), designs schedules by
feedback tracking and scenario search. Our result is complementary: it states when such searches find the global optimum and how
much they can gain.

We tested the theory with pre-registered predictions on public data from the 2018 and 2020 Otaniemi stimulations. The 2020 test refuted
our own quantitative forecast of the gain (≤ 3 % predicted, 9.1 % realised) while confirming the predicted θ-curve; we report both
outcomes. The θ-law currently rests on these two stimulations at one site, and we state this limitation explicitly. Finally, the framework
runs inside an open reservoir simulator. There we also flag where convexity is no longer guaranteed: when injection changes the fault's
permeability. That regime is the subject of a companion study.

The results are of broad interest to the induced-seismicity, geothermal and CO₂-storage communities. All data are public (CC BY 4.0).
Code, figure data and the pre-registration files will be archived on Zenodo; the pre-registrations are internal dated files, not
registry-stamped, which we disclose. The manuscript has not been submitted elsewhere. A companion reanalysis of Äspö acoustic emissions
and a simulation study of coupled faults are in preparation and do not overlap with this Letter's results.
